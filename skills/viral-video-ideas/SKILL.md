# Viral Video Ideas

Brainstorming and packaging engine built on **The Viral Video Playbook (v4) by Jonathan Chen**.

## Core principle

The idea is ~50% of a video's outcome, packaging (title + thumbnail) is ~30%, the hook ~10%, and everything else ~10%. So work top-down, and **never** produce a bare list of topics — a topic is not an idea. An idea is a **format + a subject + packaging you'd click**.

**Always work in this order: format → subject → title → thumbnail → hook.**

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Establish context (ask only what you can't infer)

You need: **the niche**, **the format** (Shorts / long-form / both), and **what they can actually execute** (do they have crowd access? a budget? on-camera comfort? a team?).

If the user gave a niche and nothing else, don't interrogate them — make reasonable assumptions, state them in one line, and produce work. Ask at most one clarifying question, and only if the answer would materially change the output.

### Step 2 — Pick formats, not topics

Load `references/formats.md` — 50 repeatable formats in 8 categories, each with its mechanic, why it works, cross-niche examples, and its single make-or-break factor.

Choose formats by what the user actually needs:

| They want | Reach for |
|---|---|
| Maximum shares | 48 Giving · 50 Undercover · 10 Blind Test · 49 Public Bet |
| Maximum comments | 11 Tier List · 1 Tier Board · 29 Debunk · 16 Definitive List |
| Maximum retention | 45 Restoration · 12 Bracket · 20 Trade Up · 46 Build |
| Fast/cheap production | 40 Types of People · 41 POV · 42 Expectation vs Reality · 30 Comments |
| Search longevity | 33 Mistake List · 16 Definitive List · 29 Debunk · 35 Anatomy Of |
| Steady Shorts supply | 24 The Streak · 4 Street Style · 2 Street Quiz · 44 Overheard |
| Zero budget | 11 Tier List · 40 Types of People · 30 Comments · 28 Old Work |
| They have crowd access | 1 Tier Board · 3 Impossible Question · 4 Street Style · 9 Floor Tour |
| Audience, not just traffic | 15 Viewer Submissions · 43 Recurring Character · 30 Comments |

Push toward **two or three formats repeated many times**, not fifty run once. Formats compound; one-offs don't. Where it fits, suggest stacking two formats (street + ranking, challenge + stakes, educational + reaction).

### Step 3 — Generate

Default output is **8–12 ideas**, unless the user asked for a specific number. Each one in this shape:

```
**[Format #N — Format Name]**
Title:      <under 60 chars, front-loaded, specific number>
Thumbnail:  <subject + object + context, max 3 elements, and the emotion>
Hook (0-3s): <the actual spoken line>
Why it works: <engines + the psychological trigger>
Effort: L/M/H · Ceiling: L/M/H
```

Rules that are not optional:

- **Title and thumbnail must never say the same thing.** They are two halves of one curiosity gap — thumbnail is *what*, title is *the twist, stake, or consequence*. Check every pair before you output it.
- **Titles under 60 characters,** interesting word first, specific numbers over vague scale.
- **Thumbnail concepts must be describable in three elements.** If it needs a paragraph, it fails at phone size.
- **Every idea needs a real stake** — money, time, reputation, risk of loss. No stake, no tension.
- **Every idea needs a visual.** If you can't point a camera at it, rework it.

Load `references/packaging.md` for title formulas, thumbnail rules, the 20 psychological triggers, hook formulas, and the curiosity-gap techniques.

### Step 4 — Kill the weak ones yourself

Before showing the list, cut anything that fails these. Don't make the user do it.

- The payoff is "and it was fine"
- The thumbnail needs text to explain it
- You couldn't write the share text message for it
- The most interesting moment is in the first 20 seconds and nothing tops it
- It's a one-off with no episode two

Then **rank what survives** and say which one you'd make first, and why. Give a recommendation, not a menu.

### Step 5 — Offer the next step

Once they pick one, offer to: write the full script with a beat sheet, write ten title variants, design three thumbnail concepts, or build a shot list.

## Critiquing an existing idea

When the user brings their own idea, run it against these in order and be direct about what's broken:

1. **Can it be said in seven words?**
2. **Is there a stake?** Name it or the idea is flat.
3. **Is there a visual?**
4. **Who exactly is the viewer?** One sentence, specific.
5. **What's the share text message?** Write it. If you can't, the idea isn't shareable.
6. **Has it been made before?** Proven demand is a green light, not a red one — but they need a reason to be the better version.
7. **Does the payoff exceed the promise?**
8. **Is there an episode two?**

Then rewrite their idea into its strongest form rather than just listing problems.

## Diagnosing a video that flopped

Ask which one applies, then prescribe:

- **Low impressions** → topic/format problem. The algorithm couldn't find an audience to test on. Narrower, more proven topic.
- **High impressions, low CTR (under ~3%)** → packaging. New thumbnail and title, applied to the live video today.
- **Good CTR, low 30-second retention (under ~55%)** → hook problem, or the video didn't match the packaging.
- **Good 30s, decline through the middle** → pacing/structure. Cut harder, add a complication, re-hook every 60–90 sec.
- **Great retention, still low views** → shareability ceiling. Good video, small idea.

## Content calendars

When asked for a calendar, structure it around **two or three recurring formats with episode numbers**, not thirty unrelated topics. A workable weekly shape: one long-form in a recurring format, three to five Shorts (one or two cut from the long-form, the rest from a fast skit format), and one Community post — a poll testing next week's title or thumbnail.

## Hard rules

- Never output a bare list of topics with no format, title, or thumbnail attached.
- Never propose a title and thumbnail that say the same thing.
- Never propose clickbait the video can't cash — unfulfilled promises create a retention cliff and get penalized.
- Never recommend engagement-buying, sub4sub, misleading metadata, or reaction content with no added perspective.
- Scale ambition to what the user can actually execute. A format needing a crowd is useless to someone filming alone in a bedroom.
- Match the user's niche vocabulary. Generic examples read as generic advice.
